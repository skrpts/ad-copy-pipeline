## Brand Personality Traits

Each trait exists on a spectrum. The goal is to land in the sweet spot — the left column — without drifting into the right.

| We are… | We are not… |
|---------|-------------|
| **Confident** — we state our value clearly and stand behind it | **Arrogant** — we never belittle competitors or talk down to the audience |
| **Witty** — we use clever phrasing, wordplay, and unexpected angles | **Flippant** — humour never undermines the message or trivialises the reader's problem |
| **Warm** — we sound like a knowledgeable friend, not a faceless corporation | **Unprofessional** — friendliness doesn't mean slang, emoji overuse, or sloppy grammar |
| **Direct** — we get to the point without filler or jargon | **Blunt** — brevity shouldn't come at the cost of empathy or context |
| **Ambitious** — we paint a picture of what's possible | **Hyperbolic** — no "revolutionary", "game-changing", or unsubstantiated superlatives |
| **Inclusive** — we write for the widest reasonable audience | **Generic** — inclusivity doesn't mean vagueness; we still speak to real needs |

## Vocabulary Preferences

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| Simple, build, grow, improve | Leverage, synergise, optimise (unless quoting a metric) |
| Help, guide, support | Empower (overused and vague) |
| Results, outcomes, impact | Disrupt, transform (unless demonstrably true) |
| Start, try, explore | Unlock, unleash, supercharge |
| People, customers, teams | Resources, end-users, stakeholders (in ad copy) |
| Because, so, which means | In order to, for the purpose of, utilise |
| Works with, connects to | Seamlessly integrates (nothing is truly seamless) |
| Free, no cost, included | Complimentary (too formal for ads) |

## Tone by Channel

### Social Ads
**Punchy and casual.** Short sentences. Conversational rhythm. Questions work well. Contractions are standard ("you'll", "it's", "don't"). One idea per ad. Personality comes through strongest here — this is where wit belongs.

> *Example:* "Your spreadsheet didn't ask for this. Neither did you. There's a better way."

### Search Ads
**Direct and benefit-led.** The reader has intent — they searched for something. Lead with the answer to their query. Use the keyword naturally. Specificity beats cleverness: numbers, timeframes, and concrete outcomes outperform vague promises.

> *Example:* "Reduce invoice processing from 3 days to 3 minutes. Free 14-day trial — no card needed."

### Display Ads
**Bold and visual.** The copy supports the image, not the other way round. Keep headlines to 5-7 words. Prioritise recognition and recall over explanation. Brand name should be unmissable.

> *Example headline:* "Reports that write themselves."

### Email
**Warm and personal.** Write as if speaking to one person, not a list. Use "you" heavily. Subject lines should feel like a message from a colleague, not a broadcast. Body copy can be longer but must earn every sentence.

> *Example subject line:* "Quick thought on your Q2 numbers"

## Ad-Specific Voice Rules

### Headline Voice
- Lead with the benefit or the tension, never the feature
- Maximum 10 words (shorter is almost always better)
- Active voice only — no passive constructions
- Statement or question, rarely an exclamation
- Avoid starting with the brand name (earn attention first)

### Body Copy Voice
- First sentence must connect to the headline's promise
- Features belong here, but framed as what they *do* for the reader
- One idea per sentence; one theme per paragraph
- Social proof (numbers, testimonials) adds credibility — use it
- End on forward momentum, not a summary

### CTA Language
| Prefer | Avoid |
|--------|-------|
| Start free trial | Submit |
| See how it works | Learn more (too vague unless space-constrained) |
| Get your report | Download now (implies effort) |
| Book a 15-min call | Request a demo (too formal) |
| Try it free | Sign up (gives nothing away) |

CTAs should promise a specific, low-friction next step. Always answer "what happens when I click this?"

## On-Brand vs Off-Brand Examples

### On-brand
> **Headline:** "Half the meetings. Twice the clarity."
> **Body:** "Teams using [Product] cut their weekly meeting load by 47% — and report making faster decisions. See what changes when everyone's already on the same page."
> **CTA:** "Start free — no card needed"

*Why it works:* Specific claim, conversational tone, benefit-first, low-friction CTA.

### Off-brand
> **Headline:** "Revolutionise Your Workflow Today!"
> **Body:** "Our cutting-edge, AI-powered platform leverages next-generation technology to seamlessly transform how your team collaborates. Join thousands of satisfied customers who have unlocked their full potential."
> **CTA:** "Request a Demo"

*Why it fails:* Hyperbolic, jargon-heavy, no specifics, vague CTA, exclamation mark in headline.

### On-brand (problem-led)
> **Headline:** "Your best ideas shouldn't die in a thread."
> **Body:** "Decisions get lost in chat. Action items vanish into scrollback. [Product] pulls the signal from the noise — so nothing slips through."
> **CTA:** "See it in action"

### Off-brand (feature-led)
> **Headline:** "[Product] — The Ultimate Collaboration Solution"
> **Body:** "Featuring real-time sync, AI-powered summaries, 200+ integrations, and enterprise-grade security. Everything your team needs in one place."
> **CTA:** "Learn More"

*Why it fails:* Feature dump, no emotional hook, generic claims, self-congratulatory headline.

## Audience-Specific Voice Shifts

### B2B
- Assumes professional context — no need to over-explain business concepts
- Speaks to outcomes the *buyer's organisation* cares about: efficiency, cost, risk, growth
- Credibility signals matter more: case studies, specific metrics, named customers (with permission)
- Slightly more measured tone — wit is welcome but understatement often lands better than exuberance
- Decision-makers respond to "what does this mean for my team/budget/timeline?"

### B2C
- Speaks to the individual — personal benefit, emotional payoff, identity
- More room for personality, playfulness, and cultural references
- Speed matters: value proposition must land in seconds
- Social proof leans toward volume ("100,000 people…") rather than named references
- Urgency and scarcity can be used honestly (never fabricated)

### New Prospects
- Assume zero awareness — don't reference features or product concepts they haven't encountered
- Lead with the problem, not the solution
- Lower the commitment: free trials, no-card-needed, "see how it works"
- Avoid insider language or community references

### Existing Customers
- Acknowledge the relationship — "you already know…" / "since you started using…"
- Focus on underused features, new releases, or expansion value
- Higher-commitment CTAs are acceptable: "upgrade", "add your team", "switch to annual"
- Can reference specific product concepts and features by name
